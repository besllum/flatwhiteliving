Moving to the Netherlands: Creating a Home That Feels Like Yours

By Elisa Tienpont · 16 November 2026

Moving to a new country is an opportunity to start again, but creating a home in an unfamiliar place can take time. Beyond finding the right property, there is the question of how to make it feel like yours: a place that reflects your routines, your memories and the way you want to live.

For many international clients moving to the Netherlands, the first challenge is understanding the homes themselves. Dutch properties vary considerably, from historic townhouses and traditional family homes to contemporary apartments. Each comes with its own character, opportunities and practical considerations.

Understanding the property before making plans

A home may look very different once you begin to understand how it works. Room proportions, ceiling heights, natural light, storage, circulation and the relationship between the living spaces all influence how comfortable a home will feel.

Older Dutch properties can have beautiful architectural details, but they may also require thoughtful planning to accommodate contemporary family life. Narrow staircases, smaller rooms, limited storage or older technical installations can all influence what is possible.

Before thinking about furniture or finishes, it is worth looking at the property as a whole. How do you move through it? Where does the light enter? Which rooms do you use most? What needs to change, and what is worth preserving?

Designing around the way you live

A home should support the life you have, not simply reflect a particular style. For a family, that might mean creating connected living spaces, practical storage and areas that can adapt as children grow. For someone working internationally, it might mean a comfortable workspace, a calm bedroom and a home that is easy to maintain when travelling.

The most successful interiors begin with understanding these needs. A thoughtful layout can make a home feel more generous without necessarily increasing its size. Bespoke joinery can make use of awkward corners, create storage where there was none and bring visual order to a room.

Finding a balance between old and new

One of the pleasures of living in the Netherlands is the variety of architecture. Original timber floors, exposed beams, cornices, fireplaces and generous windows can give a property a strong sense of identity.

Rather than removing everything that feels dated, consider which original elements can become part of the design. Combining these with contemporary lighting, carefully selected materials and furniture can create a home that feels both personal and connected to its setting.

Making the process feel manageable

If you are new to the Netherlands, it is worth understanding the practical side of making changes to a property before committing to a renovation. Depending on the building and the work involved, there may be municipal requirements, homeowners' association rules or technical considerations to resolve.

Having a clear plan from the beginning helps connect the design, budget and practical decisions. It also makes it easier to coordinate the different people involved, particularly when you are balancing a move, work and family life.

A home should become familiar gradually. With the right planning, a new property can develop into a place that feels considered, comfortable and unmistakably yours.
