Living and Renovating in The Hague

By Elisa Tienpont · 16 November 2026

The Hague has a particular atmosphere. It is an international city, but one with a distinctly residential character: leafy streets, generous townhouses, apartments, historic neighbourhoods and, of course, the proximity of the sea. For many people moving here, the appeal lies in this combination of city life, architecture and a slightly slower pace.

Finding the right home is only part of the process. Making it work for the way you live is another.

Understanding the character of your home

The Hague offers a varied mix of homes. You might find a traditional townhouse with high ceilings and original details, an apartment in a turn-of-the-century building, or a more contemporary property with a very different set of possibilities.

Each type of home comes with its own qualities and constraints. Older properties often have generous proportions and architectural details worth preserving, but their layouts may not reflect how a family lives today. Rooms can be compartmentalised, storage limited and kitchens or bathrooms in need of updating.

Before deciding what to change, it is worth understanding what already works. The position of windows, the relationship between rooms, ceiling heights and existing architectural features can all influence the design.

Designing around everyday life

A successful renovation is not necessarily the one that changes the most. It is the one that makes the home work better.

For one household, that might mean opening the kitchen towards the dining area so family life can happen more naturally. For another, it could mean creating a quieter bedroom, introducing better storage or finding a more practical place for laundry and everyday belongings.

I always begin by looking at how the people living in the home use it. Where do they spend most of their time? What feels disconnected? Where is space being wasted? These questions help establish which changes will make a meaningful difference.

Planning a renovation in The Hague

Before starting work, it is important to understand the property's status and any rules that may apply. Some homes are listed monuments; others are located within a protected cityscape or have a recognised architectural character without being formally listed.

These distinctions matter. Depending on the property and the proposed work, changes may require an environmental and planning permit. It is worth checking this early, particularly if you are considering structural changes, alterations to the exterior or work that could affect historic features.

The municipality's heritage department provides information about monument status and protected areas. The Omgevingsloket is also an important starting point for checking which rules apply to a particular address.

Making a home feel like yours

Living in The Hague means becoming part of a city with a strong architectural identity. But a home should still reflect the people who live in it.

I believe the best interiors find a balance between the character of the building and the needs of contemporary life. Original features can sit comfortably alongside modern furniture, thoughtful lighting and practical bespoke solutions. The aim is not to recreate a particular period, but to create a home that feels considered, comfortable and personal.
