A thoughtfully designed family townhouse in The Hague

Residential · The Hague

The brief

The brief was to rethink the layout of this three-storey townhouse to better accommodate the needs and lifestyle of a growing family.

The ground floor needed a new sense of purpose, allowing the living room, dining room and kitchen to function as distinct yet connected spaces. The family wanted a cosy setting for gathering around the fireplace, a more formal dining area and generous storage to display and accommodate their collections.

The upper floors also offered opportunities to make better use of the available space. The master bedroom needed to become a more complete private retreat, while the children’s rooms required additional space, practical storage and room to grow.

The overall vision was to create a warm, characterful and functional family home, combining bespoke design with high-quality materials that would stand the test of time.

The design

On the ground floor, the layout was reconfigured to give each living space its own identity while maintaining a natural connection throughout. The living room was designed around the fireplace, with a cosy seating arrangement and bespoke shelving providing space for the family’s collections. The dining room became a more defined and formal setting, while a bespoke peninsula connects the kitchen and dining area, creating a natural place for everyday interaction.

On the first floor, the master bedroom was extended towards the existing terrace, making room for a generous walk-in wardrobe and an en-suite bathroom. Steel and glass doors separate the bathroom from the dressing area, maintaining visual continuity while providing privacy when needed. The new arrangement also allowed for a reading corner filled with natural light, creating a quiet and inviting place to retreat.

The second floor was reconfigured to give the children more room to play, read and spend time together. By extending the available bedroom space, I created an additional sitting area with a sofa, making the room feel more like a little world of its own.

Storage was an important consideration throughout the children’s rooms. Bespoke floor-to-ceiling wardrobes provide space for books, toys and sports equipment, keeping the rooms organised without compromising on their playful character. Furniture was selected for its quality, practicality and timeless appeal, allowing the rooms to evolve naturally as the children grow.

Materials and details

The material palette was chosen with longevity in mind. High-quality materials and finishes were selected not only for their appearance, but also for their ability to age beautifully and develop character over time.

Oak and other natural wood finishes bring warmth, while velvet introduces softness and depth. Brushed brass adds a subtle touch of refinement, and black accents provide definition and contrast, creating balance throughout the interior. Mosaic details add texture and individuality, complementing the natural materials and carefully considered finishes.

The bathrooms offered an opportunity to introduce a more playful and expressive element. Rather than keeping these smaller spaces entirely understated, I chose wallpapers inspired by the sea, creating a personal connection to the family’s story.

Each bathroom has its own interpretation of the theme: waves in the guest bathroom, fishing nets in the master bathroom and fish in the children’s bathroom. Although different in character, the three wallpapers create a subtle visual thread throughout the house.

The result is a home that feels layered, personal and thoughtfully composed, where bespoke elements, lasting materials and individual details come together to create a warm and practical setting for family life.
