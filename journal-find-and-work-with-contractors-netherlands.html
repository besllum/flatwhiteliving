How to Find and Work with Contractors in the Netherlands

By Elisa Tienpont · 16 November 2026

Finding the right contractor is one of the most important decisions when renovating a home in the Netherlands. The quality of the work matters, of course, but so does the way a contractor communicates, plans and manages the project.

For international homeowners, this can be particularly challenging. You may not know which companies to approach, how quotations are structured or what to expect from the building process. A good working relationship starts with clear information and well-defined responsibilities.

Where to start looking

Personal recommendations are often a useful starting point. Ask people who have completed a similar renovation about their experience, and, where possible, look at the finished work in person.

You can also research specialist contractors and renovation companies through their websites, project portfolios and professional networks. Look for experience that is relevant to your project. A contractor who is excellent at bathrooms may not be the right choice for a complex structural renovation.

Before moving forward, check the company's details in the Dutch Chamber of Commerce (KVK) Business Register. Ask for references from previous clients and clarify which parts of the work the company carries out itself and which are subcontracted.

Membership of a trade association, a quality mark or a guarantee scheme can provide additional reassurance, but it is still important to understand exactly what protection it offers and whether it applies to your project.

Requesting and comparing quotations

A quotation is only useful if you can understand what is included. Before asking contractors to price the work, prepare a clear description of the project. Drawings, specifications, schedules and material selections help reduce assumptions and make quotations easier to compare.

When reviewing a quotation, look beyond the total price. Check whether it includes:

  • A clear description of the work and the areas involved.
  • Materials, quantities, finishes and installation.
  • Work by subcontractors and specialist trades.
  • Preparation, protection, removal and disposal.
  • Any provisional sums or items that have not yet been priced.
  • VAT, transport, equipment and other additional costs.
  • An indicative programme, start date and completion expectations.
  • Payment stages, warranties and the applicable general terms and conditions.

If something is unclear, ask before accepting. A lower quotation may simply exclude work that another contractor has included.

Put agreements in writing

Even when communication is friendly, important decisions should not remain verbal. The agreement should make clear what is being delivered, at what price, within which timeframe and under what conditions.

Agree how additional work will be handled. Renovations often reveal unexpected conditions once walls, floors or ceilings are opened. This does not mean every extra cost is avoidable, but it should be discussed and approved before the additional work proceeds wherever possible.

Keep a written record of decisions, revised drawings, selected materials and agreed changes. This gives everyone a shared reference point.

Communication during the renovation

A renovation involves many small decisions, often in a particular sequence. A delay in selecting a tile, approving a drawing or confirming a measurement can affect the work that follows.

Agree who your main point of contact is, how often progress will be reviewed and how urgent questions will be handled. If you are living abroad or managing the project alongside a demanding job, having someone locally coordinate the process can make a significant difference.

The importance of project coordination

A contractor is responsible for the work agreed with them. However, the wider project may involve several trades, suppliers, designers, consultants and municipal processes. Someone needs to make sure that the different parts fit together.

This is where professional project management adds value: coordinating information, monitoring progress, following up on decisions and helping resolve issues before they become larger problems.

The right contractor is not simply the one with the lowest price. It is the one whose experience, communication and approach are right for the project — supported by clear agreements and proper coordination.
