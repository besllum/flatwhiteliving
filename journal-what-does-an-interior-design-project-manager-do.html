What Does an Interior Design Project Manager Actually Do?

By Elisa Vives · 16 November 2026

A successful interior project is about much more than choosing beautiful materials or finding the right furniture. Behind every well-designed home is a process involving planning, technical decisions, suppliers, contractors, budgets and countless details that need to come together at the right time.

This is where interior design project management plays an important role. It connects the creative vision with the practical work required to make it happen, ensuring that the design is not only beautiful on paper but also achievable, functional and properly coordinated.

Turning an idea into a clear plan

Every project begins with understanding what needs to be achieved. This means looking at the property, the clients' lifestyle, their priorities and the opportunities within the space.

The design needs to be translated into something that can be understood and priced by the people who will execute it. Depending on the project, this can involve layouts, technical drawings, lighting plans, bespoke joinery details, material specifications and schedules.

The more clearly the project is defined before construction begins, the fewer assumptions need to be made along the way.

Coordinating the people involved

An interior project rarely involves just one contractor. Depending on its scope, it may require builders, electricians, plumbers, carpenters, painters, kitchen suppliers, stone specialists, lighting suppliers and other professionals.

These people need to work in a logical sequence. Some tasks cannot begin until others are completed, and decisions made by one specialist can affect the work of another.

Project management brings these different parties together, clarifies what is expected and helps ensure that everyone is working from the same information.

Managing the budget and timeline

A project manager helps translate the design into a realistic programme and budget. This includes gathering quotations, reviewing what is included, identifying items that still need to be priced and keeping track of agreed costs.

A timeline is not simply a list of dates. It needs to reflect the order of work, the availability of contractors, delivery times and the decisions that need to be made before each stage can proceed.

Material selections, for example, should be confirmed early enough to allow for production and delivery. A late decision can affect several trades and potentially delay the entire project.

Following the work on site

Once construction begins, the project moves from drawings and specifications into physical reality. This is when communication and coordination become particularly important.

The project manager follows progress against the agreed plans, helps clarify design details, coordinates information between the client and contractors and brings questions or changes to the right people.

Unexpected conditions can arise once work begins. The important thing is to understand the issue, assess its implications and agree on a solution before proceeding. Any changes that affect the design, budget or programme should be documented.

The precise level of site supervision, technical inspection and contractual responsibility should always be agreed at the beginning. Project management does not replace the specialist responsibilities of contractors, engineers or statutory building professionals.

Bringing the project to completion

The final stage is about more than seeing the work finished. It involves checking that agreed items have been completed, identifying outstanding points, coordinating corrections and collecting relevant documentation and warranties.

A proper handover gives the client a clearer understanding of what has been delivered and what may still need attention.

Design and management working together

When interior design and project management are connected, decisions can be considered from both a creative and practical perspective. A beautiful detail needs to work technically, a material needs to suit the way a home is used, and a design needs to be realistic within the available budget and programme.

At Flat White Living, I believe this connection is essential. My role is to bring the design vision and the practical process together, helping clients navigate the many decisions involved and creating a home where every element has been considered.
