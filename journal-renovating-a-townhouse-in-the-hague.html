Renovating a Townhouse in The Hague

By Elisa Tienpont · 16 November 2026

Townhouses are an important part of The Hague's residential character. Their vertical layouts, generous ceiling heights and distinctive architectural details offer many possibilities, but they can also present particular challenges when it comes to renovation.

A townhouse is not simply a home with several floors. Each level needs to work as part of the whole, while serving a different purpose in everyday family life.

Understanding how the house works

One of the first things I look at is the relationship between the floors. In some homes, the ground floor feels disconnected from the garden or the upper levels. In others, bedrooms are generous but lack practical storage, or bathrooms and circulation areas take up more space than they need.

The starting point is to understand how the household uses each level and what could work better.

The ground floor often benefits from a clear relationship between the kitchen, dining area and living room. That does not always mean creating one completely open space. Sometimes a more subtle connection between rooms offers the best balance between openness and a sense of intimacy.

Making the most of vertical space

Staircases, landings and corridors can take up a surprising amount of space in a multi-storey home. These areas deserve the same attention as the main rooms.

Storage can be integrated into otherwise underused areas, while bespoke joinery can help make awkward spaces more functional. On bedroom floors, built-in wardrobes can maximise storage and create a calmer atmosphere. In family homes, well-planned cupboards can also help keep toys, books and everyday belongings organised.

The aim is to make each floor feel purposeful rather than simply arranging rooms around the staircase.

Light and circulation

Townhouses can be long and relatively narrow, which means natural light does not always reach the centre of the home. The position of internal doors, partitions and furniture can have a significant impact on how light moves through the space.

Where appropriate and structurally possible, glazed doors or internal glass partitions can help connect rooms while allowing light to travel further into the house. Mirrors, carefully positioned lighting and a considered material palette can also make a difference.

Circulation is equally important. Moving through the home should feel intuitive, without furniture or poorly positioned doors interrupting the flow between spaces.

Choosing materials that work throughout the house

A townhouse renovation involves many rooms and often several different functions. A coherent material palette helps the home feel connected from one floor to another.

That does not mean using exactly the same finishes everywhere. Instead, a few recurring materials, colours or details can create continuity. Oak, natural stone, textured tiles, warm metals and soft fabrics can be interpreted differently depending on the room.

Durability matters too, particularly in family homes. Floors, work surfaces and upholstery need to be selected with everyday use in mind, not only how they look on completion day.

Planning the renovation

Before construction begins, it is important to establish the scope of work, understand the building's condition and identify any permissions that may be needed. If the property is a monument or lies within a protected cityscape, additional considerations may apply. Structural changes, including removing or altering walls, also need the appropriate technical assessment and checks.

A well-developed design, clear specifications and realistic programme help reduce uncertainty once work starts. In a townhouse, where trades may need to work across several floors and access can be restricted, coordination is particularly important.

Bringing the whole home together

The most successful townhouse renovations consider the property as a whole. Each room should work independently, but the transitions between floors should feel natural.

With thoughtful planning, a townhouse can retain the qualities that make it special while becoming a more practical, comfortable and contemporary home.
